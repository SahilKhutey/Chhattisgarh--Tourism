# Evidence Policy & Classification Standards

## 1. Hierarchy of Evidence
Not all interview data carries equal weight. To avoid confirmation bias ("the mom test"), all empirical data must be categorized into one of six evidence tiers:

```
T1: DIRECT_BEHAVIOR      (Highest Credibility: Observed in real-time planning/navigation tasks)
  ▲
T2: OBSERVED_WORKAROUND  (Strong: Observed reliance on complex spreadsheets, manual WhatsApp chains)
  ▲
T3: REPORTED_PAIN        (Substantial: Specific past trip frustration with tangible consequences)
  ▲
T4: USER_STATEMENT       (Moderate: General reflection on past experience or perception)
  ▲
T5: REPORTED_DESIRE      (Weak: Hypothetical future desire: "I would love an app that...")
  ▲
T6: RESEARCHER_INFERENCE (Subjective: Hypothesis or deduction formed by the interviewer)
```

## 2. Research Rules
- **Rule 1:** A problem cannot be promoted to `STRONGLY_SUPPORTED` without at least 3 distinct `DIRECT_BEHAVIOR` or `OBSERVED_WORKAROUND` evidence items.
- **Rule 2:** `REPORTED_DESIRE` is never accepted as validation for a feature. Feature justification requires proof of current pain or failed workarounds.
- **Rule 3:** Source attribution must be explicit (e.g., `INTERVIEW_TRANSCRIPT_P04_T14M`, `SCREEN_RECORD_TASK_02`).

## 3. Data Privacy & Confidentiality Rules
- Research notes must strictly avoid recording phone numbers, Aadhaar, PAN, full personal addresses, or identifying biometric data.
- Quotes published in synthesis reports must refer only to the participant's `anonymous_id` and general demographic tag (e.g., `PART-8A3F, Solo Interstate Traveler`).
